### Binance confirms $6.34bn theft by North Korean hackers, started with a fake job interview
Binance says attackers stole about $6.34bn in digital assets on 29 October after an engineer was tricked into running malware disguised as a coding assignment during a fake job interview; the FBI attributes the operation to North Korea's TraderTraitor (Lazarus). By 3 November roughly $2.85bn had been frozen or intercepted, and Binance says 98% of impacted token assets have been replenished.

### European police dismantle "crime-as-a-service" investment fraud platform; seven arrests
Europol-coordinated police in six countries (Albania, Cyprus, Finland, Germany, Kosovo, North Macedonia) arrested seven suspects and seized more than 100 servers from a company accused of supplying fake trading platforms to criminal call centres, allegedly run from Cyprus and Israel. Potential victims span at least 50 countries; no confirmed total loss figure was given for this operation.

### UK NCSC: AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure likely by 2028
Launching the NCSC's annual report, chief executive Richard Horne said the UK will likely face AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure by 2028, with AI scaling existing tactics such as ransomware rather than inventing new ones, and put the cost of malicious cyber activity to UK organisations at nearly $20bn a year. He warned that leaders who have relied on cheap, outdated technology "will have to answer for failures" if they do not invest.

### Second wave of refrigeration attacks hits 100+ supermarkets across Europe
Overnight on 1–2 November, attackers forced internet-exposed refrigeration controllers into defrost mode at more than 100 supermarkets and shops across Belgium, Estonia, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden (with reports also from Finland, Italy and Portugal), following the mid-October wave that hit about 700 French stores. No one has claimed responsibility; researchers say the targeted Danfoss, Copeland and Dixell units were reachable from the internet, many on factory-default credentials or end-of-life firmware.

### Google: stolen AI access ("LLMjacking") is costing some firms up to $1m in cloud charges
Google's threat intelligence team reports attackers are stealing cloud credentials and AI API keys, then reselling model access through "LLM mixer" services, leaving victims with the bill — over $100,000 in a single day in some cases and up to $1m cumulatively. One actor made more than 500,000 API calls in under eight hours using stolen third-party keys.

### Google dismantles XCPE botnet of 1.3 million Android devices
Google took legal action to dismantle XCPE, a botnet of more than 1.3 million Android devices — mainly TV set-top boxes, plus telehealth units and digital signage — which it says was deliberately built and operated by Chinese device maker Videostrong. Access was sold to affiliates for ad fraud, data theft, residential proxying and malware loading; Japan's National Police Agency and several security firms assisted.

### Identity vendor WorkOS breached via AI attack framework; customer sales data exposed
WorkOS, an authentication and identity platform used by at least 3,000 companies, says an attacker used the open-source ARTEX AI offensive framework — previously used against South Korean banks — to breach an internal sales application on 30 October, exposing customer contact details, account histories and excerpts of sales and support conversations. WorkOS says production services and customer-facing auth infrastructure were not affected. (Single-sourced.)
