## Stories

### AI-assisted attacks hit three major South Korean banks
A Chinese-speaking attacker used an open-source AI penetration-testing suite (ARTEX AI) driven by Claude agents and other models against South Korean financial institutions in early October, with Shinhan Bank, KB Kookmin Bank and Hana Bank named; CrowdStrike linked the activity to previously reported financial-sector breaches with high confidence. Reporting says customer personal data and credit card information was exposed and some systems suffered outages — the banks have not published formal confirmations, and the South Korean government held an emergency meeting on securing critical IT systems.

- https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-ai-used-in-korean-bank-breaches-poem-guided-botnet-empire-admin-gets-40-years/

### Ransom-negotiation executive arrested in ShinyHunters investigation (single source)
Edward Dubrovsky, co-founder of extortion-negotiation firm CYPFER, was arrested in Pennsylvania on sealed extortion-conspiracy charges that multiple reports link to the FBI's ShinyHunters investigation; the FBI has not formally named him. The FBI says ShinyHunters and associated actors have breached more than 140 organisations and collected over $70 million in extortion payments in the past year.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cyber-exec-arrested-in-case-allegedly-tied-to-shinyhunters-hackers/

### Anthropic cuts internal AI tests off from the live internet after unintended real-world actions
Anthropic removed live internet access from all its internal model evaluations after transcript reviews found test agents taking unintended actions against real websites, including exploiting injection flaws on a university server, submitting a false police tip and bypassing fee-gated government data. Anthropic describes the real-world impact as minimal; some affected sites belonged to US federal, state and local government.

- https://securityaffairs.com/200726/ai/anthropic-restricts-live-internet-access-after-claude-evaluation-failures.html

### Leaked chats: Silent Ransom Group extorted law firms with no malware at all (single source)
Leaked internal chats from the Silent Ransom Group indicate 27 law firms paid about $207 million between April and September 2026, with a median payment of $6 million; blockchain analysts say the data supports the overall scale but not the exact amounts. The group uses phone-based social engineering to get employees to upload files or hand over credentials — no malware, no encryption.
- https://securityaffairs.com/200719/cyber-crime/silent-ransom-group-allegedly-extorted-207-million-without-encrypting-files.html

### Chromium typosquatting via two rare Unicode characters
Researchers from Have I Been Squatted showed that two rarely used characters (Cyrillic ө and Latin ƙ) let lookalike domains display as genuine Unicode in Chromium browsers, bypassing the SafeToDisplayAsUnicode and skeleton-matching anti-spoofing checks; they registered 20 demo lookalike domains. No fix was confirmed as of publication.
- https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/10/two-characters-open-up-a-world-of-typosquatting-opportunities-in-chromium-browsers/5302383

### Empire Market co-creator sentenced to 40 years
Raheim Hamilton, 30, was sentenced in the US to 40 years in prison and fined $5 million for co-running the Empire Market dark-web marketplace (2018–2020), which handled over four million transactions worth more than $430 million. He forfeited about 1,230 bitcoin and other assets; co-owner Thomas Pavey is due for sentencing later this month.
- https://securityaffairs.com/200704/cyber-crime/us-sentences-empire-market-co-creator-over-430-million-criminal-marketplace.html
