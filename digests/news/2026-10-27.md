## Financial sector

### Dutch police arrest two over AI-voice bank scam that read cards through phones
Rotterdam police arrested two men accused of running a bank-helpdesk scam that used AI-generated warning calls to talk victims into installing a fake app, which read their bank card's NFC chip and relayed it to an accomplice cashing out at ATMs. Police say about 170 victims lost roughly €380,000 in three weeks; Dutch banks surfaced the fraud via the Electronic Crimes Task Force in mid-September, and police described the NFC relay method as new to them.

- https://securityaffairs.com/201786/cyber-crime/ai-generated-fake-bank-calls-and-nfc-app-enabled-e380000-fraud-dutch-police-say.html
- https://www.securityweek.com/dutch-police-arrest-two-over-nfc-relay-attacks-that-drained-400000/

### Contact-centre supplier Genesys confirms breach of internal customer-relationship data
Genesys said attackers accessed internal systems, including its CRM and learning platform, and likely downloaded names, business emails, job titles and account details used to manage customer relationships; it says customer-facing platforms were not affected and the incident, announced 19 October, was contained by 23 October. No record counts were given; a new group calling itself ScarlettGroup claimed the attack, and Genesys is warning customers to expect impersonation phishing. (Single source.)

- https://www.securityweek.com/genesys-hack-exposes-customer-data-from-internal-systems/

## Regulatory & supervisory

### US cyber agency publishes 2030 strategy promising "aggressive, anticipatory" defence
CISA's new "Defend, Harden, Prevail" strategy (published 26 October) commits the US to a more offensive-leaning, intelligence-driven posture on critical infrastructure, naming OT modernisation and supply-chain security as priorities. It imposes no new obligations; an implementation and measurement approach is promised within 180 days. (Single source.)

- https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/cisa-aggressive-strategy-critical/

## Major incidents elsewhere

### University of Copenhagen breach exposes national ID data of 525,000 people
The university confirmed attackers reached a test environment holding full names, Danish national identity numbers, phone numbers and email addresses of 525,000 people, with PIN codes of a further 20,000 staff and students also exposed. It has notified the Danish data protection regulator under GDPR and says operational systems were untouched. (Single source.)

- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/university-of-copenhagen-data-breach-exposes-data-of-525-000-people/

### Cyber incident forces New Mexico's only top-tier trauma centre to divert patients
University of New Mexico Health System diverted ambulances, cancelled appointments at more than 30 clinics and briefly left the state without a Level 1 emergency department on Monday 26 October; employees reported on-screen messages claiming theft of patient, student and staff data. The FBI is assisting and the system says services resumed in phases the same day; no attacker has been named and ransomware has not been confirmed. (Single source.)

- https://www.securityweek.com/cybersecurity-issue-forces-a-major-new-mexico-hospital-to-divert-patients-and-close-clinics/

## Active campaigns

### "Gheist" campaign uses stolen GitHub tokens to drain CI/CD secrets at scale
Wiz reports an automated campaign, tracked since 15 September, that uses stolen GitHub tokens to plant a workflow file (`sync-check.yml` on `ci-sync-*` branches) that prints every secret the CI job can read, exfiltrates it through GitHub's own logs and deletes the run seconds later — over 13,500 planted branches across more than 850 accounts, including enterprise private repositories. Tokens trace to infostealer logs and earlier supply-chain compromises; the campaign was still running as of 24 October.

### Nearly 1,000 online stores infected in year-long e-commerce malware campaign
Sansec found 953 stores, mostly on Magento, carrying injected JavaScript that silently loads an attacker-controlled payload for real shoppers while fingerprinting away bots and scanners; the campaign has run since July 2025, mostly via the PolyShell (CVE-2026-48356) and SessionReaper (CVE-2025-54236) vulnerabilities. The final payload has not been identified.

- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/hackers-infect-953-online-stores-with.html
- https://securityaffairs.com/201795/malware/magento-campaign-infects-953-online-stores-across-five-domains.html
- https://www.securityweek.com/953-online-stores-infected-in-long-lasting-malware-campaign/

### Fake IT-helpdesk chats on Microsoft Teams are ending in ransomware
Arctic Wolf describes a recurring pattern across hundreds of cases: attackers flood an employee's inbox with subscription spam, then contact them on Teams from external "Help Desk" tenants and talk them into granting remote access via tools like Quick Assist or AnyDesk, which has led to data theft and Chaos ransomware (activity tracked as STAC4749; ransomware link per Sophos). Roughly 30–40% of the sending tenants use free onmicrosoft.com domains, the rest cheap look-alike domains, mostly .top and .com. (Single source.)
