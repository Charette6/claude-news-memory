## EvilTokens phishing-as-a-service disrupted; two arrested in UK
Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit, working with UK police, Health-ISAC and SpyCloud, seized infrastructure of EvilTokens, a phishing-as-a-service platform specialising in OAuth device-code phishing that bypasses MFA; two suspected administrators (32 and 38) were arrested in London and bailed. Over 12,000 Microsoft accounts across more than 10,000 organisations were compromised since February 2026, with wholesale distribution, construction, financial services, real estate, higher education and healthcare among target sectors and the US, Canada, Australia, the UK and Saudi Arabia the most-targeted countries; the service is disrupted but not fully dismantled.

## Google fined €403m under GDPR over location data
Ireland's Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million ($463M) for processing users' location data (Web & App Activity, Location History, Location Accuracy) without adequate transparency and for retaining it longer than necessary, breaching GDPR; the investigation covered May 2018 to February 2020. Google has six months to bring the practices into compliance and says its practices have significantly evolved since 2019.

## ShinyHunters claims FBI breach via Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day (unverified)
The ShinyHunters group defaced the FBI jobs site (apply.fbijobs.gov) and claims to have stolen 2–3TB of data on FBI employees, applicants and internal systems, saying it entered through a new Oracle PeopleSoft remote-code-execution zero-day; the FBI reportedly took affected systems offline. The defacement is confirmed and 404 Media verified some of roughly 5,000 purported employee records, but the PeopleSoft zero-day, the claimed AWS GovCloud access and the data volume are not independently confirmed.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shinyhunters-claims-fbi-hack-data-theft-in-peoplesoft-zero-day-breach/
- https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/22/shinyhunters-claims-fbi-hack-this-is-not-financially-motivated/5298385

## Brevo supply-chain attack injected ClickFix scripts into customer websites (single source)
Attackers used a hardcoded Cloudflare API key with full account permissions to create malicious Cloudflare Workers on CRM/marketing platform Brevo's edge infrastructure for roughly 5.5 hours on 14 September, injecting fake verification pages, ClickFix command prompts and a WordPress backdoor-plugin installer through the forms script, Conversations widget and SDK loader customers embed on their sites; Sansec estimates up to 100,000 websites may have been affected. Brevo revoked the key, removed hardcoded credentials and says account data and email delivery were unaffected.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/brevo-supply-chain-attack-injected-clickfix-scripts-on-customer-sites/

## BigCommerce merchants' customer data accessed via compromised Ribon apps
Attackers compromised credentials for third-party Ribon applications on the BigCommerce e-commerce platform between 13 and 17 September, injecting scripts into merchant storefronts and accessing customer names, email addresses, phone numbers and shipping addresses; UK retailer Master of Malt is a confirmed victim. BigCommerce says a small number of storefronts were affected and that payment card data and passwords, stored separately, were not exposed.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bigcommerce-alerts-merchants-of-data-breach-linked-to-ribon-apps/
- https://www.securityweek.com/bigcommerce-data-stolen-via-ribon-apps-hack/

## Allied advisory on North Korean WaterPlum campaign; Japan dismantles first laptop farm
A joint advisory from Japanese, US, Australian and German agencies details the WaterPlum ("Contagious Interview") campaign, in which North Korean actors posing as employers at AI, cryptocurrency and NFT companies tricked developers and IT professionals into running malware during fake technical interviews: 30,000+ devices infected across 100+ countries (Dec 2025–Jul 2026), 7,000+ cryptocurrency wallets compromised and $10.7 million transferred to North Korea. Japan also dismantled its first North Korean "laptop farm" supporting the related fraudulent remote IT-worker scheme, with several hundred million yen in cryptocurrency traced abroad.

## ClosedQuorum: first reported malware using AI models for attack decisions
Cisco Talos documented ClosedQuorum, a Go-based Windows implant that queries Google Gemini, DeepSeek, Qwen and Mistral APIs and lets the models vote on its next action (steal credentials, inject shellcode, persist, or move laterally), exfiltrating via Discord webhooks. Talos released CAIRN, an open-source framework for tracking AI-integrated malware; the analysed sample carried placeholder credentials and no confirmed in-the-wild deployment was documented.
- https://blog.talosintelligence.com/the-closed-quorum-inside-the-first-reported-autonomous-ai-c2-implant/

- https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/22/cairn-open-source-framework-ai-malware-closedquorum/
