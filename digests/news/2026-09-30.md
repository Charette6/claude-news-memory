## Stories

### Citrix NetScaler zero-days exploited against banks and governments
Attackers exploited two critical NetScaler zero-days (CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, both CVSS 9.5) for weeks before Citrix's 29 September disclosure, planting a custom web shell (WHIPSHOT) and a tunneling tool (SLAPSHOT) on compromised appliances. Google, Mandiant and GreyNoise confirm victims in financial services, government, education and professional services across North America and Europe, with exploitation ongoing since at least early September and now escalating into mass attacks.

### US tech firms sign voluntary AI self-policing accord (single source)
Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, Nvidia and xAI signed a voluntary accord on AI development oversight: robust internal controls, independent external audits, board committees to review audit reports, and support for communities hosting data centres. The accord is not legally binding but explicitly states these steps may later be codified into laws and regulations.

- https://www.securityweek.com/trump-says-top-tech-firms-have-signed-accord-to-self-police-ai-development/

### US defence personnel agency breach exposes 3 million records
A vulnerability in a file-sharing system at the Pentagon's Defense Manpower Data Center allowed access to an unencrypted server holding personnel records — Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth and contact details for 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased individuals. The flaw was open from October 2025 until discovery on 16 July 2026, roughly nine months, and no group has claimed the attack.

- https://www.securityweek.com/pentagon-personnel-agency-data-breach-impacts-3-million-people/
- https://securityaffairs.com/200017/uncategorized/three-million-affected-in-pentagon-personnel-agency-data-breach.html

### French tax authority: stolen staff passwords went undetected for seven weeks (single source)
Attackers used several dozen staff passwords, likely harvested by infostealers over three months, to log into two DGFIP portals that required only a password, stealing tax data on more than 350,000 individuals and 250,000 businesses between June and August 2026. The theft went unnoticed for seven weeks until the attacker announced it; multi-factor authentication was added only afterwards.

- https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/french-tax-data-theft-using-stolen.html

### Two Japanese railway operators disclose weekend cyber incidents
Keio Corporation confirmed a ransomware attack on 26 September that shut down business systems (trains kept running), with a possible leak of business and customer information still under investigation and no group claiming it. Separately, Tokyo Metro reported unauthorized access via compromised email credentials exposing about 59,000 loyalty-programme email addresses.

- https://securityaffairs.com/200027/data-breach/japanese-railway-operators-keio-corporation-and-tokyo-metro-disclose-security-breaches.html
- https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/japanese-railway-operators-cyber/

### ShinyHunters leader arrested; FBI tells remaining members to surrender
Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man on 15 September alleged to be a ShinyHunters leader, and the FBI publicly urged remaining members to turn themselves in. The extortion group has hit more than 140 organizations — largely via corporate single sign-on accounts, third-party vendors and platforms like Salesforce and Snowflake — collecting a confirmed $70 million in extortion payments.

### Microsoft details NeedyMantis, a persistence framework used by China-based actors
Microsoft dissected NeedyMantis, a modular post-compromise framework in use since at least October 2025 by China-based actors, including the group behind the Daemon Tools supply-chain attack, to keep long-term access to compromised networks. Reported targeting covers telecommunications, government, universities, contractors and intergovernmental organizations.

- https://www.securityweek.com/daemon-tools-hackers-needymantis-malware-dissected-by-microsoft/
