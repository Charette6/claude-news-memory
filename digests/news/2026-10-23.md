## Financial sector

### Hacker claims leak of 375,000 State Bank of India customer records — unconfirmed
A threat actor calling itself Siriusim claims to hold 375,000 CSV rows and 2,722 PDF documents allegedly from SBI, including loan applications and account-opening forms; the claim surfaced on 16 October and gained wider coverage in this window. SBI has not confirmed any compromise, and the authenticity, origin and extent of the data remain unverified.

## Regulatory & supervisory

### US court orders Meta to pay $25 billion over Facebook privacy deception
A New Mexico judge entered a $25 billion penalty after a jury found Meta liable for more than 43 million consumer-protection violations for misleading users about control of their data, rooted in the Cambridge Analytica harvesting of roughly 87 million profiles. Meta will appeal; the award dwarfs the 2019 $5 billion FTC fine and the $18 billion multistate settlement Meta reached in August.
- https://www.securityweek.com/meta-ordered-to-pay-new-mexico-25-billion-for-deceiving-users-about-privacy-on-facebook/
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/meta-ordered-to-pay-25-billion-after.html

### EU opens DSA investigation into Booking.com over phishing and scam risks (single-sourced)
The European Commission opened formal proceedings under the Digital Services Act over whether Booking.com adequately mitigated phishing risks — including how it shares reservation and guest data with accommodation providers — and whether its messaging service can be abused for fraud. Non-compliance can draw fines of up to 6% of global annual turnover.
- https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/eu-bookingcom-scam-risks/

## Major incidents elsewhere

### Capcom confirms breach of 11.7 million customer accounts (single-sourced)
The Japanese games publisher confirmed attackers accessed its CAPCOM ID system, exposing more than 11.7 million customer email addresses with login metadata; attackers changed passwords on 196 accounts to lock owners out, while payment card data was unaffected. Capcom detected the intrusion on 19 October and reported it to police and Japan's privacy regulator by 21 October.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/capcom-confirms-data-breach-affecting-117-million-accounts/

### UPDATE: eMudhra exits the public certificate business after weak-key scandal
The Indian certificate authority announced it is withdrawing from the global SSL/TLS business after eight of its intermediate CA keys were found to be guessable and Chrome, Firefox and Apple blocked them; revocation services will run until issued certificates expire, and Google is reviewing whether its remaining roots stay trusted. India's CERT-In warned the weak keys could enable credential theft and financial fraud, while the company insists there was no breach.
- https://therecord.media/indian-tech-company-announces-withdrawal-certificate-business-incident
- https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/22/disgraced-indian-cert-issuer-leaves-the-cert-issuing-business/5304848
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/emudhra-to-exit-global-ssltls-business.html
- https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/emudhra-certificate-compromise/

### Cloud platform Railway warns customers their stored secrets may be exposed
Railway, a deployment platform with about 4.8 million users, said an intruder may have accessed customer environment variables and obtained decrypted contents for some, potentially exposing API keys and database credentials; it paused deployments and told all users to rotate secrets. The company says the intrusion was part of a series of attacks on AI inference providers.

## Active campaigns

### UPDATE: Microsoft pins ConnectWise supply-chain attack on Chinese state actor using AI agents
Microsoft attributed the ConnectWise compromise to Storm-0940, a Chinese government-aligned group, which used ARTEX — an open-source AI penetration-testing framework — to target hundreds of organisations through the breached vendor, gaining privileged cloud persistence at many; finance is among the sectors breached, and earlier ARTEX activity hit South Korean and Japanese financial institutions. Microsoft observed under three hours from initial targeting to privileged access, with over 111,000 failed log-ons on 11 October alone.

### LiteLLM release pipeline hijacked to push credential-stealing malware — second time this year
Attackers compromised the release pipeline of LiteLLM, a widely used AI gateway (about 92 million monthly PyPI downloads), and published a malicious version 1.107.0 to PyPI, Docker Hub and GHCR for roughly two hours on 21 October; it steals cloud, CI/CD and AI-provider credentials and installs a persistent loader. The project was also compromised in March via its CI tooling; users of 1.107.0 are told to rebuild and rotate all secrets.
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/litellm-release-pipeline-hacked-to-distribute-info-stealer-malware/

### INTERPOL operation across Africa: 1,462 arrests, $105 million recovered
Operation Serengeti III, spanning 20 African countries, arrested 1,462 suspects, recovered more than $105 million and disrupted over 10,000 malicious networks tied to online fraud, business email compromise, ransomware and investment scams, with nearly 66,000 victims identified. Several investigations remain open.
