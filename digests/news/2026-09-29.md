## Financial sector

### Bitget confirms its $388m theft began with a zero-day in a third-party security product
Crypto exchange Bitget said the attacker behind the 24 September theft of roughly $388m exploited a zero-day in an unnamed third-party security product to obtain high-level internal credentials, then inserted fraudulent withdrawal commands that bypassed risk controls. Customer balances are covered by the exchange's protection fund, Bitcoin withdrawals resumed 28 September with other assets to follow by 2 October, and Bitget suspects North Korean actors.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/bitget-says-attacker-exploited-third.html
- https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/bitget-restarts-withdrawals-387-5m/
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bitget-resumes-bitcoin-withdrawals-after-3875-million-crypto-heist/

### Android banking trojan service uses AI to rank victims by estimated bank balance (single-sourced)
Researchers at Cleafy detailed RatHat, a malware-as-a-service Android banking trojan that overlays fake login screens on banking apps and whose operator console uses Google's Gemini to estimate each victim's bank balance from stolen text messages, sorting infected phones into high- and mid-value groups. Nearly 100 console deployments have been observed since April 2026, spread via SMS and malicious ads.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/rathat-android-malware-console-uses.html

## Regulatory & supervisory

### New Mexico jury finds Meta deceived users about privacy; penalties could reach billions
A jury found Meta violated New Mexico's Unfair Practices Act nearly 44 million times by falsely telling users they controlled data sharing and that it did not sell their information to advertisers; each violation carries up to $5,000 in civil penalties, with a judge to set the final figure in the coming weeks. The state is also seeking court-ordered reforms to Meta's data practices.
- https://therecord.media/facebook-new-mexico-privacy
- https://www.securityweek.com/new-mexico-jury-finds-facebook-liable-for-deceiving-users-about-privacy-protections/

### Former US soldier sentenced to 70 months for hacking and extorting AT&T and nine other firms
Cameron Wagenius, 22, a former US Army soldier, received 70 months in prison and $295,000 restitution for hacking and extorting AT&T and at least nine other companies during 2023-24, including attempting to sell stolen data to a foreign military intelligence service. He ran the operation while on active duty, working with at least three co-conspirators.

### Japanese car-sharing service Times Car confirms breach of 6.6 million accounts (single-sourced)
Times Car (Park24 group) confirmed that attackers with access to its systems from early September stole data on about 6.6 million current and former members, including names, addresses, driver's licence details and identity-verification document images; card data was not taken. Access was cut off on 26 September and a forensic investigation is under way.

## Active campaigns

### JadePuffer used service-principal credentials leaked on GitHub to destroy a victim's Azure estate
Attackers linked to JADEPUFFER (Microsoft: Storm-3168) used Azure service-principal credentials an employee had exposed in a public GitHub issue — removed, but still visible in edit history — to run roughly 300 reconnaissance reads and then 150+ destructive operations in 35 minutes, deleting most of the victim's storage accounts and targeting key vaults and databases. Resource locks blocked some, not all, of the deletions.

### ShinyHunters still mass-exploiting Oracle PeopleSoft; suspected member arrested in Amsterdam
Mandiant warns ShinyHunters has resumed mass exploitation of the Oracle PeopleSoft flaw patched in June, specifically targeting organisations that applied published workarounds but not the patch itself; dozens of systems across government (including the FBI's still-offline jobs portal), education, healthcare and IT services carry web shells. Separately, Dutch police arrested suspected member Pepijn van der Stap, a previously convicted hacker, in mid-September.

### Carbonato botnet installs a Telegram-controlled AI agent on exposed Docker hosts
A worm-like botnet is compromising Docker daemons exposed without authentication on port 2375, launching privileged containers and installing an AI agent that receives tasks over Telegram, generates its own commands via an LLM and prioritises credential theft; it scans neighbouring networks every five minutes to spread. The operators, apparently Costa Rica-based, remain unattributed.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/carbonato-botnet-compromises-docker.html
