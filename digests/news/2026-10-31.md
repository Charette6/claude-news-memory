## Stories

### Binance loses ~$6.3 billion in the largest crypto theft on record
On 29 October roughly $6.36 billion left Binance wallets across ten blockchains in about 20 minutes; Binance traced the intrusion to a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop that gave access to a service container for unauthorized withdrawals, with no evidence of a wallet private-key leak. TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute the attack with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor (Lazarus); about $2.7–3 billion has been frozen by stablecoin issuers, and Binance says all customer losses will be covered from its SAFU fund.

### Polymarket traders drained ~$2–2.7 million through a poisoned monitoring script
Polymarket halted trading on 29 October after malicious JavaScript, served through the compromised real-user-monitoring snippet of UK vendor DebugBear, sold logged-in users' positions or redirected their tokens to an attacker address using the victims' own authenticated sessions. Loss estimates from researchers range from about $2.2 million to $2.7 million; Polymarket resumed trading within an hour, removed the script and pledged to reimburse affected traders.
- https://www.securityweek.com/2-million-drained-from-polymarket-traders-in-new-supply-chain-attack/
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/polymarket-halts-trading-after-crypto-draining-script-breach-25m-traced/

### UK oversight body faults GCHQ for thousands of unaccounted-for storage devices
The UK's Investigatory Powers Commissioner's annual report says GCHQ cannot establish the scale, location or recoverability of removable media it has lost track of — GCHQ's own estimate is "in the thousands" of USB sticks, drives and discs — and criticizes how long the agency took to investigate issues first raised years ago. GCHQ says its media-tracking system was not sufficiently robust, frames the problem as flawed records rather than lost items, and says it has completed an audit and acted on all recommendations.

- https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/30/british-intelligence-agency-misplaced-thousands-of-storage-devices-years-later-still-cant-say-how-many/5306605

### Florida student financial aid site breach exposes data of 4.9 million people
Florida's Department of Education disclosed that an unauthorized party accessed the state student financial aid website, exposing names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth and Social Security numbers of nearly 5 million applicants. The breach was detected on 19 October and disclosed on 29 October; no attacker has been named and the state says the incident is contained. (Single source.)
- https://therecord.media/florida-warns-5-million-affected-financial-aid-site-breach

### Chinese state actor runs AI-orchestrated intrusions against 200+ organizations
CrowdStrike reports that China-linked Vapor Panda has, since 17 October, used ARTEX — an AI framework that coordinates LLM-driven agents for reconnaissance, exploitation and lateral movement under human oversight — against more than 200 organizations, including foreign ministries across several regions and 19 US organizations in sectors including finance; post-exploitation was confirmed at nine victims. Observed techniques leaned on identity weaknesses: password spraying, SAML assertion abuse, and admin tokens forged from authentication keys unrotated since 2022. (Single source.)

### Fake IT help desk callers succeed with nothing more than names and email addresses
Cisco Talos' Q3 incident-response report describes attackers posing as IT help desk staff over phone, email and Teams, talking employees of any seniority into opening remote-access sessions — in one case leading to ransomware deployment after a Quick Assist session. Talos found no deep victim profiling behind the calls, and notes insufficient logging in over 40% of engagements. (Single source.)
- https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/30/cisco-talos-it-help-desk-vishing/

### Suspected Chinese actor roots Palo Alto firewalls and reaches a US government network
A suspected Chinese nation-state actor used the leaked PHP2Shell exploit chain, tailored to PAN-OS and combined with a Linux kernel flaw, to root 24 internet-exposed Palo Alto firewalls in the US, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea on 23–24 October, gaining persistent Active Directory access in a US government-affiliated environment; Unit 42 verified the findings. The chain completes in under 15 minutes against exposed management interfaces; vulnerability specifics are covered in the Vulnerability Brief.
- https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/chinese-us-government-chained-php2/
- https://www.securityweek.com/php2shell-attacks-root-palo-alto-firewalls-but-fail-against-wordpress/
