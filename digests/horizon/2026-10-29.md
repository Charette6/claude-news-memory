### Google's new frontier model claims top exploit scores and goes to vetted defenders first
- Slug: `gemini-4-carbon-cyber-gated-fairwind`
- Origin: feed (DeepMind blog, Ars Technica, The Verge)
- Type and confidence: announcement, moderate confidence (all figures are vendor-reported)
- Summary: Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model to date and the successor to Gemini 4 Argon. Without guardrails, Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (vulnerability finding), 54.8% on ExploitGym (exploitation) and 71.0% on CWE-bench (patching). It also claims more than twice Argon's success rate at secure code generation. Access starts with a subset of cyber defenders and expands to trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, with broader availability once guardrails have been iterated. Google says it takes part in US government voluntary pre-release testing. It reports 0.0% attack success on Gray Swan's prompt-injection benchmark.
- Editorial note: the brief's claim that this is the "third major lab in about five weeks to gate a model on cyber capability" is our own inference from earlier archive entries (Opus 5.5 on 2026-09-22, Gemini 4 Argon on 2026-10-01, Meta Muse Wonder on 2026-10-13). It is not stated in the source.

### AI-tooled intrusions linked to the Korean financial attacks reach a UK registrar as Korean firms are hit again
- Slug: `123-reg-ai-intrusion-artex-korea-financial-wave`
- Origin: feed (The Register, TechCrunch)
- Type and confidence: event, moderate confidence (attribution is suspected, not confirmed)
- Summary: 123 Reg, owned by GoDaddy, disclosed an "AI-driven cyberattack" on 18 September. Investigators found AI attack skills on Chinese infrastructure, including copies of skills Anthropic had open-sourced, along with other AI exploitation frameworks. The attacker read customer contact and relationship data, VAT numbers and product data. 123 Reg suspects a Chinese state-sponsored group that abused Claude Code against about 30 organisations, or the group that used the ARTEX agentic pentest tool against South Korean financial institutions. Separately, Daishin Securities disclosed that data on 38,490 customers was exposed, and Mirae Asset Global Investments and Bithumb reported incidents. The root cause of this latest Korean wave is still under investigation, and no AI link has been established for it.
- Sources: https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/28/123-reg-discloses-ai-cyberattack-on-customer-data-suspects-china-nexus/5305936 , https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/south-koreas-financial-industry-hit-by-fresh-wave-of-cyberattacks/

### Cisco open-sources Project Quarry to detect harmful AI agent behaviour across a whole session
- Slug: `cisco-project-quarry-agent-session-detection`
- Origin: feed (The Register)
- Type and confidence: announcement, moderate confidence (no performance data)
- Summary: Project Quarry is a behaviour-detection engine that evaluates whole agent sessions, including multi-turn LLM conversations, tool use, MCP interactions and multimodal inputs. It uses rules modelled on YARA and Snort and can flag sequences in which each step looks benign on its own. The project is on GitHub with plugins for Claude Code, Codex and other MCP agents. Cisco says rules do not replace chain-of-thought monitoring or human oversight. It has not run Quarry against data from the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident.
- Source: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/28/cisco-open-sources-tool-for-detecting-ai-agents-on-hacking-sprees/5305867
