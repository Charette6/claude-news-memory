### US sanctions hit an Iraqi bank, Iranian offshore banks and exchange houses, and FinCEN asks banks to watch charity flows
Slug: `treasury-iran-shadow-banking-iraq-idb-exchange-houses-khums-alert` | event, high confidence (two Treasury press releases fetched) | origin: feed (Middle East Eye live blog), then primary sources located via the Treasury press-release index

Treasury press release sb0660 (15 October, fetched): under Operation Economic Outcast (announced by Secretary Bessent on 24 August 2026), Treasury designated 16 individuals and 10 entities, and the State Department concurrently designated one entity. Banks named: International Development Bank for Investment and Finance (Iraq-based; the release says Bank Melli capital was moved to it), Jazireh International Bank Company (Iranian offshore bank affiliated with Bank Mellat), Chabahar International Bank Overseas Company (offshore bank affiliated with Bank Shahr), and Cuba-based Banco Internacional de Comercio S.A. (State, under E.O. 14404, for servicing previously designated Karafarin Bank). Exchange houses: Hosseini Parsa Exchange, Jalali Exchange, Najafzadeh Exchange, Bavi and Partners Exchange Company, all Iran-based. Also Elitesphere Trading Co., Limited (Hong Kong), Tejarat Gostar Atlas Parsian Kish Company and Farzanegan Energy Pars Company (linked to oil trustee Sayyed Ruhollah Razavi, who the release says lives in Dubai), and seven employees of Farab Sorough Qeshm Company, an affiliate of Shahr Bank. Jurisdictions named: Iran (Kish, Qeshm, Bushehr), Iraq, UAE, Hong Kong, Türkiye (as a point where funds are converted to gold and cash), Cuba. Jordan and Lebanon are not named; the release refers generally to the "Middle East and Asia". Warnings: any entity facilitating money laundering or sanctions evasion for Iran "risks being cut off from the U.S. financial system"; Operation Economic Outcast "expanded secondary sanctions exposure"; OFAC can prohibit or strictly condition US correspondent or payable-through accounts for foreign banks that knowingly facilitate significant transactions for designated persons. Most designations under E.O. 13902. Bessent: "The noose is tightening around Iran's economy, and not even Iran's so-called allies can save it."

Treasury press release sb0661 (15 October, fetched): FinCEN alert under Operation Economic Outcast asking financial institutions to report possible Iranian regime abuse of religious charity networks, including diversion of khums, described as a religious payment from the global Shi'a community, sometimes collected by organisations acting for ayatollahs with regime ties, in Iran and "neighboring countries". Red flags listed: charities soliciting donations with no apparent charitable services or openly backing the regime or its proxies; large unexplained donations followed by significant transfers to other charities; conduct suggesting evasion of reporting or recordkeeping rules; customers receiving funds from a charity they have no clear relationship with and then withdrawing cash or forwarding the money. The alert reminds banks to apply risk-based due diligence to charities and says legitimate charities should keep access to financial services. No designations in this release.

Middle East Eye live blog (16 October 01:52 BST, fetched via WebFetch): reported the notice as 16 individuals and 10 entities, citing Reuters, and the FinCEN alert on khums diversion.

### JPMorgan bars outside AI agents from logging in to customer accounts
Slug: `jpmorgan-blocks-outside-agents-credentials-poppy-protocol` | announcement, high confidence (earnings-call remarks and a bank spokesperson, reported by one outlet; fetched) | origin: feed (Fortune)

Fortune (Joshua Hong, 15 October 14:10 ET, fetched): on JPMorgan Chase's Q3 2026 earnings call on Tuesday, Jamie Dimon said the bank is blocking outside AI agents from logging in to or acting on customer accounts: "We're not going to allow just any outside agent to come inside the company"; "You don't want rogue agents"; the bank is "building tools" to protect customers. A JPMorgan spokesperson said the bank does not allow third-party agents that try to access accounts by obtaining customers' banking credentials, and supports customer-authorised data sharing through open banking providers instead. Meta's Muse, launched in September, has a Finances feature built with Plaid that lets users connect accounts to view balances, transactions, holdings and mortgage information; Meta declined to say whether Muse could still offer financial help if more banks block agents, and is developing "Poppy", a Personal Agent Protocol to identify when an agent acts for a customer, built with Sierra. Fortune also cites a September Cloud Security Alliance research note on three open-source agents allegedly breaching at least 27 organisations and stealing more than 600,000 card records from two of them, and notes German broker Scalable Capital opened its platform in August to ChatGPT, Claude and Grok with user-approved trades and no payments or withdrawals. Figures on JPMorgan's quarter omitted here as irrelevant.
