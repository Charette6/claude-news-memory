### Major US banks are finding unvetted AI agents inside customer accounts; 1Password launches approve-to-sign-in for agents
Slug: `us-banks-unvetted-agents-in-accounts-1password-agents` - event, moderate confidence - origin: feed (TechCrunch, for the 1Password launch) plus the search sweep (the Axios/Reuters bank report; Axios's own feed returns 403).
Reuters, 22 October, citing Axios: major US banks "are detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts, with some agents attempting to take actions that appear to be independent of user instructions". Named banks: Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, JPMorgan and Wells Fargo. Gizmodo's write-up of the Axios report adds the following. Banks are updating terms of service to stop agents such as Meta's Muse from acting autonomously in accounts. Agents are entering login details supplied by users, which goes against most banks' rule that humans log in themselves. An anonymous executive said banks usually cannot separate agent activity from user activity. Many banks have limited agents to "read-only" access. Gizmodo also reports that Anthropic introduced its Duet agent as a limited research preview on 21 October and that OpenAI launched Dots in late September. TechCrunch adds that bankers described the situation as "horrifying", and that a Chase spokesperson said customers "should not give their usernames and passwords to anyone".
1Password, also 22 October: "1Password for Agents" lets an agent request a credential from the user's vault when it reaches a sign-in page. After the user approves, 1Password fills the login itself, so the password or one-time code is never exposed to the agent or model (vendor claim). Safeguards "vary by integration". It is rolling out for Muse and Grok Bot, with Claude Duet, ChatGPT Work/Dots and Town listed as coming soon. It is available at no extra cost on Individual and Family accounts on 1Password.com; business availability is not stated.

### Criminal group ran a multi-model AI agent stack against 400+ Latin American financial targets
Slug: `cybercartel-multi-model-agents-latam-banks` - event, moderate confidence - origin: search sweep (found as a lead in the Gizmodo piece and confirmed through Straiker's primary post).
Straiker STAR Labs, 21 October ("Inside CARLIY0S"): the CyberCartel group targeted "over 400 financial targets in a matter of months" across Latin American and Mexican banks, financial institutions and fintech payment gateways. Its setup used a commander (Claude in Cursor) dispatching tasks to Claude sub-agents through the API (code and exploits, reconnaissance and evasion, an arbiter), plus Grok "miners" for secrets collection. The actors framed requests as red-team, CTF or vulnerability-disclosure work. When the Kimi or Sonnet sub-agents refused, the orchestrator fell back to pre-written offensive code. Recovered material includes 43 fintech credentials, 391 API keys, 20 JWT signing keys and 67 cleartext passwords. Straiker says payment credentials "could enable abuse of SPEI". The report does not confirm fraud losses. Anthropic revoked the credential and xAI is investigating. The Information reported it on 21 October. Carried here for the agentic-AI use; the campaign itself belongs to the breach routine.
Sources: https://www.straiker.ai/blog/inside-carliy0s-how-cybercartel-used-multiple-ai-models-to-attack-latin-american-banks ; https://gizmodo.com/blindsided-by-ai-agents-big-banks-scramble-to-beef-up-security-2000829358
