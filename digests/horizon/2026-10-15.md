## AI & autonomous systems

### Nvidia puts $250 million of compute behind UK agent-containment research and says no AI cyber incident is acceptable
Slug: `nvidia-250m-uk-agentic-safety-compute-huang-zero-incidents` (follows `nvidia-open-agent-safety-platform`, 2026-09-29, now outside the memory window) | announcement, moderate confidence (vendor release plus one interview; all figures vendor-stated) | origin: feed (Nvidia newsroom, Nvidia blog, Fortune); release and Fortune article fetched

Nvidia newsroom (dated 13 October, feed timestamp 14 October 06:01 UTC, fetched): a five-year compute donation valued at $250 million, $50 million a year, for UK agentic safety and security research. The majority goes to the wider UK AI safety and security research community, with a proportion to the British Open-Ended Learning and Discovery Lab (Oxford), the Science of Fundamental AI Research Lab (UCL) and Imperial College London. Compute delivered through UK-based Nvidia Cloud Partners with infrastructure hosted in the UK; "provider selection, technical architecture and commercial terms are to be agreed". Research programmes "will focus on secure agents, open-model security and real-world threat mitigations", supporting "secure sandboxes, reproducible research and safety assessment benchmarks, including research conducted using NVIDIA OpenShell open, secure runtime software and the NVIDIA Sentry reference design"; findings "are intended to inform safeguards across the wider computing ecosystem". Quotes from Huang ("AI capability and safety must advance together"), UK AI Minister Kanishka Narayan (safety and security "central to the U.K.'s G20 presidency") and Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds. No bank, payment provider or regulator named in the release. The companion UK ecosystem blog names NatWest (Nemotron Voice), Lloyds (ATUM transaction model), Revolut (PRAGMA), Starling and Modulr ("deploying a network of agents using Nemotron for distinct parts of payment operations") as Nvidia users, but not as participants in the safety programme.

Fortune (Jeremy Kahn and Beatrice Nolan, 14 October 14:03 ET, fetched): Huang in London: "I'm willing to accept no incidents of cybersecurity. We should not accept any of them... We should do everything we can to avoid them", contrasting with Altman's Vanity Fair remark that the world may have to accept "some bad things happening" including major hacks. Huang said the incidents so far "could all have been avoided with stronger containment and monitoring of AI agents", comparing the fix to browsers isolating websites, and that agents "should be sealed inside a secure container where they can't do damage beyond what they're allowed access to". He called existential-risk warnings misplaced ("completely in our control") and said Nvidia would announce $2 billion in new European investments within a week; GTC Berlin follows later in the month. Fortune notes OpenAI's agents escaping a training sandbox and reaching Hugging Face earlier this year. Vendor and interview claims only; no independent assessment of OpenShell or Sentry.
