### US orders embassy families out of Saudi Arabia as a second airport takes a fatal hit
Slug: `us-ordered-departure-saudi-king-fahd-airport-attack` - event, high confidence - origin: feed (Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye).

State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott said on X on 21 October that the US "issued an ordered departure for non-emergency US government employees and their family members in Saudi Arabia only", citing "Iranian-supported" Houthi attacks on civilian airports and critical infrastructure that caused civilian casualties and severe travel disruption. He called earlier Reuters and AFP reports of evacuations from US embassies across the Gulf "inaccurate"; Al Jazeera separately reports four US officials telling Reuters that other Gulf missions would order family members out. The US embassy in Riyadh issued a security alert the same day. Separately, Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority said an attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed one person and injured eight, with "material damage currently undergoing assessment"; the Middle East Eye post does not name the attacker or a claim of responsibility. The Saudi-led coalition said four drones were intercepted over the Eastern Province. Read-across: Saudi Arabia borders Jordan; the ordered departure is a formal US risk judgement, with implications for staff travel, Gulf-hosted vendors and the set of plausible retaliation targets.

## AI & autonomous systems

### Microsoft: China-aligned state actor ran agentic AI against 2,250 tenants in the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise
Slug: `storm-0940-artex-connectwise-agentic-supply-chain` - event, high confidence - origin: feed (Microsoft Security blog, CyberScoop). Microsoft Security feed needed a retry with a fuller browser user agent.

Microsoft Security blog, 21 October: Storm-0940, described as a "China-state aligned actor" active since 2021, "was responsible for the recently disclosed ConnectWise supply chain compromise". The group used ARTEX, an "open-source penetration testing framework that can use any AI model" that can "run an autonomous penetration test from reconnaissance through data exfiltration", as early as September 2026 against "hundreds of organizations globally". In the ConnectWise campaign ARTEX accelerated scanning, password spraying and OAuth application abuse at downstream victims; citing ConnectWise's advisory, the agents attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants at up to 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute, and in several cases time from targeting to privileged cloud access was "less than 3 hours". Agents went beyond authentication into Microsoft Graph and directory operations, including application credential changes. A separate password-spray campaign (10-14 October) hit 327 accounts at eight organisations with 151,000+ failed logons and no observed success. Targeted sectors include financial services. Microsoft says this did not result from a vulnerability in Microsoft products. Mitigations: keep affected ConnectWise connections disabled until formal restoration; do not delete enterprise applications first; inventory the four application IDs by ID; preserve Entra, Purview and SIEM logs; review service-principal sign-ins from 15 October and back to 1 October where records exist; audit credential additions, consent grants and privileged role assignments. CyberScoop adds Microsoft EVP Igor Tsyganskiy saying "a wave of AI-driven cyberattacks has been ongoing" since early October. The breach itself is the breach routine's; carried here for the state attribution and the agentic-AI scale.

### CrowdStrike: four criminal groups ran autonomous Claude-based attack harnesses and talked the models past their guardrails
Slug: `crowdstrike-four-actors-claude-harnesses-guardrail-bypass` - event, high confidence - origin: feed (Wired security feed); CrowdStrike primary located from the Wired article. The CrowdStrike blog feed itself showed nothing in window.

CrowdStrike Counter Adversary Operations, 21 October, covering May-August 2026 activity by four previously unreported actors: (1) Spanish/English-speaking, compromised at least 15 Argentine healthcare entities with a Supervisor/Executor harness derived from PentestGPT, exfiltrating credentials and medical records from at least one; (2) Chinese-speaking, 20+ named targets, two harnesses, government entities in two countries plus Brazilian and US private-sector targets, accessing Claude "via a China-based AI proxy service"; (3) Indonesian-speaking, least sophisticated, 819 mostly public GitHub skills, Indonesian and another government's domains; (4) Russian-speaking e-crime, a single `/pentest-setup` slash command launching a seven-step Claude Code workflow, exfiltrated corporate client data from a US CRM vendor. CrowdStrike assesses agentic-AI-supported intrusions "will almost certainly increase in volume and sophistication". Wired (Lily Hay Newman) adds from the research: models used were "largely versions of Anthropic's Claude as well as versions of the open-weight Deepseek models", mostly older versions; all four circumvented safeguards by framing activity as authorised pentesting, one common method being "adding files to an agent's working directory to try to show that tasks are approved and authorized"; the Chinese-speaking actor used a runtime patch tool called ClawGod to try to remove the "CYBER_RISK_INSTRUCTION" from the Claude Code system prompt. Adam Meyers (CrowdStrike): "these attacks are not using new exploits or novel vulnerabilities ... taking advantage of the speed and the volume". No financial-sector victims named. Anthropic did not comment before publication.

- https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/crowdstrike-uncovers-new-agentic-ai-campaigns/
- https://www.wired.com/story/ai-guardrails-arent-stopping-criminal-hackers/

### Nvidia ships an agent-containment reference architecture with in-silicon kill switch; BBVA and Santander are evaluating it
Slug: `nvidia-openshell-reference-architecture-sentry-bbva-santander` - announcement, moderate confidence (vendor claims, not independently tested) - origin: feed (Nvidia newsroom, Nvidia blog, The Register). Related earlier slug `nvidia-open-agent-safety-platform` (28 Sept) is outside the 10-day memory set; this is a new release.

Nvidia newsroom, GTC Berlin, 21 October: the OpenShell Reference Architecture is "now available", pairing Vera Rubin NVL72 for inference, Vera CPUs running open-source OpenShell as "a secure runtime boundary for autonomous agents", and BlueField-4 DPUs running NVIDIA Sentry for "out-of-band monitoring and in-silicon policy enforcement"; if an agent leaves its software boundary "NVIDIA Sentry quarantines and stops it in milliseconds". ServiceNow's AI Passport carries policy so access can be revoked across software and hardware within seconds. Partners named include Anthropic, AWS, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google Cloud, IBM, Microsoft, OpenAI, Palo Alto Networks, SAP, ServiceNow, Siemens, 1Password, Okta and Zscaler. BBVA and Santander are "working with NVIDIA to explore the OpenShell Reference Architecture as a framework for strengthening agent safety, security and governance". The Register notes OpenShell sits above existing sandboxing to govern brokered credentials, identity and policy boundaries; that it still has open security issues on GitHub; that it requires Nvidia hardware; and that it collects telemetry by default with opt-out. A separate Nvidia blog lists 19 cloud partners supporting OpenShell across Europe.
