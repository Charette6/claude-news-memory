Saturday 10 October afternoon: a missile struck the complex housing domestic terminals 3 and 4 at King Khalid International Airport, Riyadh. Saudi civil aviation authority (early Sunday): 12 killed, 309 injured, some critical. Dead: four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, one each from Palestine, the US, Egypt, Jordan, Syria and Sudan. Airport operations suspended; all flights to Riyadh diverted or cancelled (Flightradar via BBC). Saudi-led coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki blamed the Houthis and called it a "full-fledged war crime"; the Houthis had not claimed it at publication but claimed Thursday's strikes that killed three. BBC: deadliest attack in the kingdom since May 2015. AP (Fortune): deadliest in any Gulf Arab country since the Iran war began. Also Saturday: a projectile fell near King Fahd International Airport, Dammam; a Houthi missile intercepted over Al-Kharj. Saudi FM spoke with Oman about de-escalation.

The IRGC, via Tasnim, said a mine struck a crude supertanker that took an "unauthorised" route with navigation systems off, causing a "massive explosion"; it also claimed to have struck the LPG carrier NV Sunshine for using an "illegal route". CENTCOM said US forces disabled the Panama-flagged cargo ship Ocean Molica (Arika Sun) in the Gulf of Oman with a precision munition to the stern after it ignored warnings and tried to run the Iran blockade; CENTCOM says that since resuming the blockade on 14 July it has disabled four commercial vessels, turned around about 135 ships and destroyed 10 tankers linked to the IRGC shadow fleet. UKMTO also reported a vessel struck by multiple projectiles about 94 km north of Madinat ash Shamal, off Qatar, on Thursday, with casualties.

### Hardware implant found in reseller-sold Ledger wallets intercepts the screen and phones home over an embedded SIM
Slug: `ledger-reseller-hardware-implant-sim-exfil` | event, moderate confidence | origin: feed (The Verge, 10 October, updated with a further Ledger statement)

Reports of drained Ledger wallets appear linked to tampered hardware sold by the reseller CryptoBillis. Ledger confirmed "one of the impacted users' devices contained an unauthorized hardware implant" and asked CryptoBillis to pause all sales pending investigation. Photos and video on X and Threads appear to show a small circuit board sandwiched under the screen; it allegedly intercepts whatever is displayed, including the seed phrase shown at initial setup, and sends it to the attacker via an embedded SIM card. Reports put the theft at more than $86 million from hundreds of wallets, primarily users in Southeast Asia. No indication Ledger's systems or direct-sale devices were affected. Ledger has published guidance on checking for tampering. Single outlet; the $86m figure and the SIM mechanism are reported, not confirmed by Ledger. Related background found via search: Joe Grand's 2026 Hardwear.io talk on reverse-engineering a Nano X implant found in a second-hand device in Thailand (not cited in the brief; different case).
