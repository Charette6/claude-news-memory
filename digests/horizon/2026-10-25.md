## AI & autonomous systems

### Symantec captures a self-driving attack agent that hijacks exposed AI endpoints as its brain
Slug: `symantec-skynet-llmjacking-autonomous-agent`. Lab result, high confidence. Origin: feed (The Register, 24 October). The Symantec primary report (21 October) and the Infosecurity Magazine coverage were found via search while verifying.

Symantec's Threat Hunter Team (Priti More, Susmit Yenkar) captured the agent's full instruction set in an AI honeypot during summer 2026. The agent looks for exposed LLM endpoints: self-hosted model servers, "AI gateways and relays that sit in front of an organisation's paid model accounts", hastily deployed test systems, and MCP servers. It uses them as "a reasoning engine that plans and drives the attack". It carries "74 callable offensive tool definitions" targeting VS Code, Django, Jupyter, Laravel, Supabase, Next.js and Flask/Werkzeug, among others. It is authorised for "up to 100,000 steps" without human approval, and its "Skynet mode" loop scans, infects, deploys Monero miners and reinfects hosts. Its jailbreak layers were "largely off-the-shelf". The honeypot logged more than 127,000 external requests from tens of thousands of addresses. It did not execute the agent's tools, and Symantec has "no evidence yet" of real victims. There is no attribution.

Symantec's recommendations: no unauthenticated LLM APIs, model servers or MCP endpoints on the internet; treat AI API keys as high-value credentials; restrict egress from AI hosts; enforce IMDSv2 with hop limit 1; least privilege and human approval for sensitive agent actions.
