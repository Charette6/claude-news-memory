### Binance says attackers used its own internal AI tool to move laterally before the $6.3bn theft
Slug: `binance-internal-ai-tool-lateral-movement`. Event, high confidence (primary source). Origin: feed (Verge, Register, BBC, Nextgov, Wired, TechCrunch). The breach itself belongs to the incidents routine; this entry covers only the AI-tool angle.

Binance's "29 October 2026 Binance Security Incident: Initial Findings and Response Update", dated 3 November, says that on 20 October the attacker launched the attack with the "use of an internal AI tool as a springboard". After compromising Software Engineer B's computer, the attacker used the tool to take control of the machines of Wallet DevOps Engineer A and Wallet Software Engineer C. Engineer A's computer held a container-deployment permission token that was later used in the theft. Remediation includes "Rebuilt the affected internal AI tool and access controls" and enhanced AI-based behavioural anomaly detection. The post does not describe the tool or its permissions. The Register quotes the report as "used the compromised computer and an internal AI tool to gain access to the computers of wallet DevOps engineer A". The FBI attributes the attack to TraderTraitor (DPRK), per The Verge.

Read-across: internal AI assistants that can reach employee endpoints have now been used in practice as a lateral-movement path. Their reach and credentials belong in the same review as privileged admin tooling.

### UK NCSC: states will routinely use AI-enabled intrusion by 2028
Slug: `ncsc-ai-cyber-intrusion-2028`. Forecast, high confidence that this is the NCSC's stated judgement. Origin: feed (NCSC).

The NCSC assessment "Artificial intelligence and cyber intrusion" (3 November) makes these judgements:
- It is highly likely that Russia, China and other states will routinely use AI-enabled cyber capability.
- It is almost certain that many systems will remain vulnerable to AI tools that find and exploit weaknesses, and cheap replacement of lost access will make attackers less cautious.
- It is likely that attackers will target legacy CNI technology and the attack surface created by AI tools deployed within CNI.
- It is highly likely that AI-powered malware will be used in a ransomware attack on UK CNI by 2028.

The assessment uses baseline, pessimistic and optimistic scenarios. Russian actors are assessed to have adapted open-source models. It was launched alongside the NCSC's State of the Cyber Nation review: £60m for a Cyber Shield, £70m for a NERV centre, and an AI Cyber Security Centre of Excellence in Manchester. It does not name the finance sector.
