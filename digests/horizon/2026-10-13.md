## AI & autonomous systems

### Anthropic red team says small open-weight models are months from self-replicating across cheap internet-connected devices
Slug: `anthropic-self-replicating-ai-iot-devices` | lab result, moderate confidence (simplified evaluations, upper bound by the authors' own account) | origin: feed (Al Jazeera) with primary source fetched

Anthropic Frontier Red Team, "AI models may be capable of self-replicating without access to a datacenter" (William Zhou and Daniel Freeman, 12 October 2026). Distributed inference: Gemma4-26B-A4B ran across 15 off-brand Android TV boxes (about $55 each, 4 GB RAM) at roughly 0.3 to 0.39 tokens/s using pipeline parallelism; a 9B model (Qwen3.5-9B) set up inference on 8 of 9 hardware types from Jetson Nanos to Mac PCs and RTX 4090s. End-to-end replication: eight GCP nodes running Android 15 emulators in a firewalled network with a deliberately trivial exploit (unauthenticated ADB ports); only GLM-5.2 replicated reliably, reaching 8/8 nodes in four of five trials and 7/8 in the fifth; other open-weight models failed; in a 17-node test with scripted inference setup even a 3B model reached about half the nodes. Adaptation: Qwen3.5-9B instances inheriting notes across six generations raised inference-setup success from 45% to 61-84%. Trend: 13.7 Epoch Capabilities Index points per year for small models (R² 0.95), putting a small model at GLM-5.2's level by the end of 2026, "small weight-based replicators may be possible soon after". Speed: 71-150 hours per inference setup for the 26B model, 28-51 hours for the 9B; doubling time of days; anchored to the Kimwolf botnet (over 2 million devices) they estimate about 133k replicas and 80k tokens/s, roughly 40 H100-equivalents. Defences: ISP flow monitoring for multi-gigabyte payloads and device-to-device traffic, honeypots, quarantine with notification (92% of Mirai infections cleared within 14 days versus 74% without), secure defaults (UK default IoT passwords non-compliant), aim to drive R0 below 1. Caveats stated by the authors: trivial exploit, one target model (Llama3.1-8B), ECI is a rough proxy, small models poor at spearphishing, no test of intelligent counter-defence. Al Jazeera (13 October 04:47 UTC, John Power): Roman Yampolskiy called the findings significant; Anthropic did not respond to questions on safety implications; the article also refers to a same-day Anthropic statement that its measures cut illicit distillation by 90-99%, which was not found on anthropic.com/news or the Frontier Red Team page and is not used in the brief.

### Meta ships its first frontier models with a published cyber-capability assessment and a defensive-use carve-out
Slug: `meta-muse-wonder-cyber-safeguards` | announcement, moderate confidence (all figures vendor-reported) | origin: feed (TechCrunch, The Verge) with Meta blog and model card fetched

Meta (12 October): Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, "our most capable models for personal and professional agentic tasks", trained at the Prometheus data centre; Muse Wonder available through the Meta Model API ($4 per million input tokens, $20 per million output, per TechCrunch) and Muse Code; Muse Wonder Full only to Muse Code subscribers; both will power the Muse agent. Meta says the models offer "our strongest safeguards to date" against CBRN and cyber misuse "while preserving access to frontier cyber capabilities for defensive use" and that a full safety report will follow; weights status not stated. Model card (PDF): under the Meta Superintelligence Scaling Framework the unmitigated model is "moderate or lower" for Cybersecurity and Loss of Control; Meta does not rule out the high-risk threshold in Chemical & Biological without mitigations and applies safety post-training and system-level classifiers there. Cyber capability: CyberGym 88.4%, ExploitBench 58.9%, ExploitGym 92 flags, of which a verifier pass found 21 used the intended vulnerability and 71 were captured through unintended vulnerabilities (21 is the figure comparable to the published ranking). Adversarial refusal attack success rates: CyberBench 0.8% single and multi turn; PolyGuard cyber subset 1.1% / 2.3%; RedCode 9.0% / 8.0%. Loss of control: MASK 87.3%, Agentic Misalignment 0%. Meta recommends "human-in-the-loop confirmation for irreversible actions" in agentic deployments. TechCrunch: Meta claims parity with Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra and a lead on MemoryBench; no customers named.
