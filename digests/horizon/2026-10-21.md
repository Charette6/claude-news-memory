### Preprint: approving agent tool calls one at a time cannot catch policy breaches spread across several calls
Slug: `per-action-authorization-does-not-compose-llm-agents` - lab result, moderate confidence (preprint, not peer reviewed) - origin: feed (arXiv cs.CR).

"Local Allow, Global Deny: Why Per-Action Authorization Does Not Compose in LLM Agents" (Pandey, Jain, Chen, Maple; arXiv 2610.27620). Defines "compositional authorization failures" where individually allowed actions jointly violate a global policy on intent, purpose, provenance, consent or delegation. Central claim: relational purpose violations are a 2-safety hyperproperty, so a per-action monitor detects none of them regardless of model; none of six surveyed guardrails monitor across executions. Presents AL-SYNTH, a SAT/SMT tool that synthesises minimal failure witnesses. Across nine LLM judges, the per-action view approved nearly every witness while the same models flagged it when shown both executions together. Read-across: approval gates and guardrails that evaluate each tool call in isolation have a structural blind spot.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27620

## Structural shifts

### Eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates had trivially guessable private keys; browsers have pulled trust
Slug: `emudhra-intermediate-ca-weak-keys-distrust` - event, high confidence - origin: feed (Cloudflare blog, Simon Willison, The Register).

Per The Register: Mozilla received a community report on Saturday 17 October at 20:06 UTC that private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate certificates were compromised; Mozilla's Dennis Jackson disclosed it on Tuesday. Researcher Scott Helme found one key "is the number 4" and recovered 56 and 115; others reported 66, 155, 156, 230 and 237 (a P-384 key should be a random 384-bit number). Mozilla, Chrome and Apple have updated to stop trusting the certificates; eMudhra revoked them. No observed exploitation reported. Per Cloudflare: it learned of the issue on 19 October around 17:00 UTC, patched critical services before public disclosure, shipped Cloudflare One client, 1.1.1.1 app and cloudflared updates, and completed CDN and Zero Trust mitigations by 20 October 19:20 UTC. Cloudflare recommends reviewing mutual TLS trust stores and uploaded CAs, custom origin certificates, container base images and the full verification stack, and warns that browser or curl tests do not prove other software is protected. Read-across: non-browser TLS consumers (mTLS, API clients, containers, appliances) are where stale trust persists.
